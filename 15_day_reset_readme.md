# 15-Day Reset: Money, Skills, Startup & YouTube

> **Mission:** Turn scattered effort into visible outcomes. The
> immediate priority is the ₹1.5 lakh college-fee deadline in 15 days;
> the long-term priority is becoming employable in AI/ML and building
> independent income.

## Reality check

₹1.5 lakh in 15 days is an urgent and difficult target. A new YouTube
channel or an internship application is not a reliable way to earn that
amount on this timeline. Do not build the plan around one uncertain
source.

Run two tracks together:

1.  **Cash and deadline track:** pursue paid client work, advance-paid
    small projects, genuinely paid internships, and a fee
    extension/installment arrangement with the college.
2.  **Career and creator track:** keep DSA moving, improve one existing
    AI project, and turn the work into useful YouTube content.

Do not count an application, interview, verbal promise, or unpaid task
as income. Count money only when payment terms are agreed and payment is
received.

## 1. Priorities for the next 15 days

### Priority A --- Handle the fee deadline today

-   [ ] Contact the college accounts office/administration and ask about
    an installment plan, extension, late-fee policy, emergency
    assistance, or scholarship options.
-   [ ] Ask what exact amount is due immediately to avoid a penalty or
    academic issue.
-   [ ] Write down the deadline, minimum amount needed by that date, and

**Important:** Freelance work can bring money sooner than a typical
hiring process, but no plan can guarantee ₹1.5 lakh in 15 days. Keep
negotiating the deadline while pursuing income.

### Priority B --- Sell skills you already have

You have experience with AI agents, RAG, FastAPI, React, APIs, and
deployed projects. Do not start another course before offering a focused
service.

Offer small, clearly scoped services such as:

-   Fixing and deploying a broken React/FastAPI application.
-   Adding a document Q&A/RAG feature to an existing app.
-   Connecting an API, authentication, database, or AI endpoint.
-   Building a simple internal AI workflow or lead-processing
    automation.
-   Debugging and deploying a prototype for a founder or small business.

Start with a specific outcome, not a vague offer like "I can do AI/ML."
Use your deployed projects as proof. Quote after understanding the
scope; ask for a written agreement and a reasonable advance before
starting. Never promise a delivery date you cannot meet.

#### Daily outreach target

-   [ ] Send 10--15 tailored messages to founders, small agencies, local
    businesses, alumni, or people already seeking a developer.
-   [ ] Follow up on earlier conversations.
-   [ ] Share one relevant demo, GitHub repository, or deployed project
    with each suitable lead.
-   [ ] Record the lead, need, quoted price, next follow-up, and payment
    status in a simple spreadsheet.
-   [ ] Prefer paid, bounded work over unpaid "test projects" that
    produce no portfolio value.

A useful outreach template:

> Hi \[Name\], I noticed \[specific issue/opportunity\]. I build
> AI-enabled web apps using React, FastAPI, APIs, and RAG. I can help
> with \[one concrete outcome\]. Here is a relevant demo: \[link\]. If
> this is useful, I can first confirm the scope, delivery date, and
> fixed price. Would you be open to a quick discussion?

Personalize every message. Do not spam the same message to hundreds of
people.

### Priority C --- Apply to paid internships selectively

-   [ ] Apply to a small batch of relevant, recently verified openings
    each day.
-   [ ] Prioritize AI engineering, GenAI/RAG, backend, and full-stack
    roles that match your current projects.
-   [ ] Reuse a clean base resume and a short project portfolio. Keep a
    private document with common profile answers so forms take less
    time.
-   [ ] Fill required fields honestly; do not invent experience, impact,
    or skills.
-   [ ] Track the date, role, link, pay, deadline, status, and next
    action.
-   [ ] Verify that each opening is still active on the employer's
    official page before spending time on a long application.

Do not spend an entire day perfecting a profile form. Set a time limit
for each application and move on when a form is disproportionately long
or the role is a poor fit.

## 3. How to work with unreliable hostel internet

Prepare work that can continue offline:

-   Download DSA problem statements, notes, and reference material when
    connected.
-   Keep your project repository, setup instructions, and a small task
    list available locally.
-   Batch uploads, deployment, applications, and messages during
    reliable connectivity.
-   Write code and test locally where possible; queue non-urgent online
    actions for the next connection window.
-   Keep a local `TODAY.md` with the exact file, bug, or task to resume.
    Avoid repeatedly asking an AI coding tool to redesign the whole
    project.

### Antigravity / unpaid internship boundary

Before accepting more work, clarify the deliverable, expected hours,
mentor/reviewer, learning value, and whether there is a real path to
paid work. If it remains unpaid and does not offer meaningful learning,
references, or portfolio value, cap the time you give it and use the
freed time for paid work. Do not silently abandon a commitment;
communicate your boundary professionally.

For every AI-generated code change: 1. Read the diff. 2. Understand what
changed. 3. Run the relevant test. 4. Commit only when the change works.
5. Write one sentence explaining what you learned.

## 4. DSA: keep the habit, make it measurable

Two questions a day can be enough for steady progress when you learn
from them properly.

For each problem: 1. Try independently for 20--30 minutes. 2. Write the
brute-force idea first. 3. If stuck, use a hint before watching the full
solution. 4. Implement without copying. 5. Note the pattern, complexity,
and mistake. 6. Re-solve it the next day without the video.

For the current graph focus, use a sequence such as: - Grid boundaries
and four-direction movement. - Number of Islands using DFS. - Number of
Islands using BFS. - Rotting Oranges using multi-source BFS and
level-by-level time. - Connected components in an adjacency-list graph.

Do not count a question as learned just because the video finished.

## 5. YouTube: use the work you are already doing

Do not wait for a perfect channel identity, camera, or editing setup.
Start with screen recordings and honest progress updates. Do not reveal
private company code, client information, credentials, or internship
materials.

### First video idea

**Title:** "I'm a 3rd-Year AI Student Trying to Fix My Productivity and
Build Income"

Possible structure: 1. **Hook (0--15 sec):** "I keep working every day,
but at night I still feel like I achieved nothing. For the next 15 days,
I'm changing how I work." 2. **Context (15--45 sec):** Explain the
challenge without sharing private financial or college details you do
not want public. 3. **Show the work:** Record yourself solving a DSA
problem, fixing a real bug, or building a small AI feature. 4.
**Result:** Show what actually worked and what did not. Never fake
results or earnings. 5. **Next episode:** State one measurable goal for
the next day.

### Low-friction weekly content

-   1 weekly progress video.
-   2--3 short clips cut from coding, debugging, or lessons learned.
-   Record while working; do not create a separate, elaborate production
    workload.
-   Publish consistently, but do not expect YouTube to solve the
    immediate fee deadline.

## 6. Your startup: validate before building more

You have an AI job-search/application product idea. Keep it small until
you know which part users actually need.

For the next 15 days: - \[ \] Write a one-sentence description of the
target user and problem. - \[ \] Talk to 5 people who actively apply for
internships/jobs. - \[ \] Ask how they currently find jobs, where they
lose time, and what they would trust automation to do. - \[ \] Show a
simple flow or clickable prototype. - \[ \] Pick one core workflow to
improve based on feedback. - \[ \] Track whether users return or agree
to test it.

Do not spend days adding a load balancer, scaling for 1,000 users, or
building every feature before validating the core workflow. For now,
keep the app reliable for one user, use real job links, and avoid
claiming an application was submitted unless it truly was.

    interview.
